# MJ Adom — Issues with the Current Website
### Internal Review Note | July 2026

---

## Background

The current website was built on the initial understanding that MJ Adom's primary business was cocoa trading under its Licensed Buying Company (LBC) licence. As a result, the site was designed and structured around a single commodity and a single service. Following a broader review of the company's full strategy and business direction, a number of significant issues have been identified that require the website to be rethought from the ground up.

---

## Key Issues

### 1. Identity Is Too Narrow
The current site presents MJ Adom as a cocoa trading company. This is inaccurate and limiting. MJ Adom is a diversified commodities and investment company with active and planned operations across multiple sectors — agriculture, logistics, processing, export, and strategic investment. A cocoa-first identity misrepresents the company and undermines its credibility as a serious, multi-sector business.

### 2. It Locks the Company Into One Commodity
By centering the website on cocoa, any future service addition — cashew, shea, minerals, logistics, investment — will feel like an afterthought or a rebrand rather than a natural extension of who MJ Adom already is. The site's current structure cannot accommodate growth without a significant overhaul each time.

### 3. It Misrepresents What the Company Actually Does
MJ Adom is not purely a cocoa buyer. The company operates across aggregation, logistics, processing, export, agribusiness development, and strategic investment. None of these are reflected in the current site, which means anyone visiting it walks away with an incomplete and misleading picture of the company's capabilities.

### 4. It Creates the Wrong First Impression for Investors and Partners
The company's ambition — as outlined in its 2026–2030 strategy — is to become one of Africa's most trusted commodities and investment companies. A commodity-specific website does not communicate that ambition. Investors, strategic partners, and institutional buyers visiting the site will not see a company they can grow with. They will see a cocoa trader.

### 5. It Cannot Reflect Licences and Services as They Come Online
Part of the company's current situation is that several licences and services are still being finalised. The current site has no structure to accommodate the phased rollout of new services. There is no "coming soon" framing, no modular services section, and no flexible architecture that allows updates without breaking the existing design.

### 6. It Does Not Communicate the Company's Values or Long-Term Story
The current site says what MJ Adom sells, not who MJ Adom is. There is no articulation of the company's values, its commitment to sustainability, its support for farmers and communities, or its 5-year vision. For a company trying to attract serious investors and long-term partners, this is a significant gap.

---

## Summary

The current website was built for a narrower version of MJ Adom than actually exists. It served as a useful starting point but is not fit for purpose as the company's public-facing identity. The new website needs to lead with a broad, credible corporate identity — one that reflects the full scope of what MJ Adom is building — and present individual services as components within that larger story, not as the story itself.
